BLACK PEOPLE

Black people refers to a diverse global population encompassing individuals with a mid- to dark-brown complexion, a racial classification most commonly associated with people of African ancestry, though it also historically includes Indigenous populations from regions like Australia, Melanesia, and parts of Asia. The identities within this global diaspora are incredibly varied, spanning distinct ethnic groups, nationalities, and cultures, such as African Americans in the United States—who largely descend from enslaved people brought from West and Central Africa—as well as Afro-Caribbeans, Afro-Latinos, and continental Africans. Throughout history, Black individuals and communities have profoundly shaped global culture, pioneering foundational musical genres like jazz, blues, rock and roll, reggae, and hip-hop, while making monumental contributions to literature, science, politics, and the visual arts. This rich cultural heritage is deeply intertwined with a history of resilience; generations have fought against systemic inequities, transatlantic slavery, colonialism, and segregation through era-defining efforts like the Civil Rights Movement, the anti-apartheid struggle, and modern human rights advocacy. Today, the preservation and celebration of this history are honored globally through observances like Black History Month and Juneteenth, which highlight both the enduring challenges and the extraordinary achievements of Black people worldwide.
